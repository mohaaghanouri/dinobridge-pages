How we work

We take the repeating work. You keep the judgement.

You get back the hours that repeat every week, and keep every decision that carries your name. Our clients arrive with different job titles and the same sentence: one part of my week has become unbearable.

Read more

You do not need to know what to automate; almost nobody does. You know what hurts. Our job is to find the exact line between the work that repeats and the work that needs your judgement, and to build only on the repeating side — finding that line is the expertise you are hiring.

Clear tests decide what we take over.

Repetitive work — all four must be true

  • It repeats. More than once a week, in roughly the same shape.
  • It follows rules. If you had to explain how you do it, you could, in sentences rather than gestures.
  • It is easy to check. A wrong result is obviously wrong to you within seconds.
  • A caught mistake costs little. If the tool gets it wrong and you notice, you lose a minute, nothing more.
we build up to here

Your judgement — any one is enough

  • You are liable for it. Your name, licence or signature is on it.
  • Your taste is the product. People pay you precisely because the view is yours.
  • The relationship is the product. A patient, a student, a client, a reader.
  • It is a real decision under uncertainty, not a lookup with extra steps.

Nearly every failed AI project we have been asked to rescue crossed that line, and the people using it quietly stopped trusting the results. We build up to it and no further.

Four steps, and your team runs it at the end.

  1. 01
    First: we learn your business.

    How you win work, where the money leaks, what your people spend their days on, and what you have quietly stopped believing is possible. Not a workshop, not a questionnaire. We sit in the building and ask questions.

  2. 02
    Then: one page shows where AI pays off for you.

    The services you could offer, the speed you could reach, the costs you could cut — and what stays with your people, because your name, your judgement or your relationships are on it. We read it back to you, and you approve or reject each line before anything is built. That page is the plan.

  3. 03
    Then: we prove the first ideas in your business.

    Two or three ideas from the page are tested in your office, with your data, in your own accounts. What works stays. What does not costs you an afternoon and teaches you something.

  4. 04
    Last: your team takes over, and we decide what is next.

    The people who will run what works learn to adjust it without us, and each system gets a named owner on your side. Then we look at the page together and decide whether we stay on as your Head of AI for the months ahead.

We tell you when AI will not pay off.

An honest no saves you money, and it is most of the reason clients introduce us to other owners. These are the five cases where we advise against building.

There is too little of it.

Under roughly an hour a week, keeping a tool running costs more than the task itself.

Every case is different.

If no two instances look alike, there is nothing repetitive to take over. There is only skill, and skill is not a bottleneck to remove.

One mistake would be too expensive.

If a single wrong result could end a career, the tool prepares the material and does nothing else.

A simple tool already exists.

A calendar, a form, a shared spreadsheet, a template. We set that up, say so, and move on.

It is a decision, not a workload.

Some people do not need a system. They need to decide something they have been avoiding. No software fixes that.

Common questions.

What happens after we get in touch?

A free thirty-minute call, with no preparation on your side. You describe your business, and we tell you honestly where AI would change something for you and where it would not. If it makes sense, you receive a written proposal at a fixed price; if not, you leave with a clear recommendation, which happens about one time in three.

Is our data safe with you?

Yes, and for regulated work it is the first thing we design. Everything runs in your own accounts, under your own logins, and you can withdraw our access in an afternoon. Before we see anything, we sign an NDA, a data processing agreement under Art. 28 GDPR and a one-page scope of access — and for law firms, a confidentiality undertaking under § 43e BRAO. Your content is never used for training, and the security page sets out the rest.

Who actually does the work?

A senior Dinobridge team, led by its founder, Moha Aghanoori. The people on your first call are the people who work with you, set up the tools and train your team; nothing is passed to people you have never met. Behind that are years of building AI systems that other people depend on.

I am one person, not a company. Is this for me?

Yes — most of the people we have worked with came this way. You bring a question, whether a decision, an idea, a company to start or something to learn, and get a session or a few in plain language, at a fixed price agreed in writing. The first conversation is free, and if AI will not help with your question, you hear that in it. The page for individuals sets out how it runs.

Can you take the Head of AI role for us?

Yes, that is the usual next step. After the consulting work we take the role on a monthly retainer: direction, experiments, tool decisions in writing, your AI policy kept current, leadership briefings and a fixed weekly slot your team can rely on. You can stop with one month’s notice, and nobody on your side needs technical knowledge.

We already have IT. What do you do that they do not?

IT keeps your systems running; we ask what the business could do with AI that it cannot do today, and test the ideas that survive with your people. We work with your IT team, not around them. If they want to own the result, even better: we spend the first weeks with them, and you stop paying us sooner.

We already use ChatGPT, or we have Copilot. Why would we need you?

Good — your people are willing. It usually also means company material is going into a tool nobody approved, with no record of what went in. A licence helps with the sentence in front of you; it does not see that your contracts get lost in email or that every deadline lives inside a document. We work on the shape of the work, and on clear rules for what may go into any tool.

Could we not just hire an AI engineer?

You could, and if you can already describe the role, you probably should. Most businesses at this stage cannot, and an engineer without a clear brief builds what they find interesting. Start with the consulting work: if the answer is to hire someone, we say so, help you write the job description and gladly step back.

Will the tool make decisions for us?

No, and that is the point of how we build. The advice stays with the partner, the valuation with the estate agent, the direction with the founder; the tool takes over the repetitive work and prepares the material. Your people decide — we have declined to build an “accept all” button more than once.

You have never worked in our field. Does this still work?

Usually, yes. What we build changes completely from field to field, but how we find it has not had to: we learn the business, map where AI helps and where it must stop, and run the first experiments. If your field turns out to be the exception, you hear that early, not at the end.

Our people will resist this.

Some will, usually the ones with good reason: fear for their job, or a past tool that made the work worse. So we build only where the person doing the work agrees it is repetitive, we never build anything that reports on individuals, and the decision always stays with a person. In the team-leaders example we were asked twice for a version that watched people, and declined both times.

How would we prove the return?

We agree the numbers before we start: two or three measurements with today’s values, such as hours on a named task or days from request to result. At the end we measure the same things again, and if they have not moved, the write-up says so. A quick test: count the task for one week, multiply by what that person’s hour is worth and halve it — if the number is small, we will tell you it is not worth buying.

Are we locked in to you?

No, and that is built in, not promised. Everything runs in your own accounts, under your own logins, and belongs to you; each system has a written handover and a named owner on your team who has already changed it themselves. Nothing depends on a licence, account or subscription of ours, and if we disappeared tomorrow it would all keep running. We will put that in the contract.

What if we want to stop?

The retainer ends with one month’s notice, any month, without a reason. Everything we built keeps running in your accounts, and you get the handover for each system and, on request, a full export of the records and written confirmation that we hold nothing of yours. The consulting work has a natural end, and you keep the plan and the working systems either way.

What if AI is the wrong answer for us?

Then you hear it in the first conversation, before you commit to anything. It happens often: what looks like an AI problem is a process nobody owns, or a tool your team already has and does not use. If we only see it once we are inside the business, we say so, stop, and bill only for the days already spent.

Your founder also has a full-time job. How does that work?

Moha Aghanoori, our founder, also holds a full-time engineering role building AI that people depend on, and that work is where much of the experience you are hiring comes from. The work for you is done by the Dinobridge team he leads, and we take a small number of clients at a time so each one gets that team’s full attention. Which days, on site or by video, and over how many weeks is agreed in writing before there is a contract.

Do we have to be in Berlin?

No. The first conversation is a call either way, and the Head of AI role runs partly over video once we know your business. Clients so far include Leipzig and Brandenburg; Berlin simply makes the time on site easier.

How much of our time does this take?

Most of it falls in the consulting work: time with us in your business, plus an afternoon of training for the people who will own what we build. After that, the Head of AI role takes a short weekly meeting with your leadership and the occasional hour from your team. What we put in place takes seconds to use: ninety seconds of dictation after a site visit, one photograph of an invoice.

Let’s talk about what AI could do for you.

Thirty minutes, free, no preparation. We tell you honestly where AI could change something for you, and where it would not.
