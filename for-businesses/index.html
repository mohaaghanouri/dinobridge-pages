Time back. Better decisions. Room to grow.

Most businesses meet AI as a chat window somebody remembers to open. We set it up so the routine work runs on its own and your people spend their hours on the work only they can do.

Routine work, done before your team arrives.

Work that used to wait for someone to have a free hour now happens on its own, in your own accounts, and is ready when your people sit down. The estate agents we work with now answer every buyer within minutes instead of hours.

One capability for the whole team.

One shared system for everyone whose work touches the same material, so what one person works out, everybody gets. That is the difference between buying licences and having a capability. In the law firms we work with, partner time per incoming contract fell from about forty minutes to eleven.

Better decisions, still made by you.

It does the groundwork you never have a spare week for: the market nobody checked, the seven offers nobody compared, the option that was never on the table. You get the options and the reasoning behind them, and you choose. One founder-CEO now prepares for his board in a morning instead of a weekend.

Room for new work, not only shorter weeks.

Some of what we find makes an existing week shorter; some of it becomes work you could not have taken on last year. One non-profit now writes a grant application in three hours instead of two days, and submits twice as many. We test every idea in your own business before you commit to it.

Four steps, and your team runs it at the end.

  1. 01
    First: we learn your business.

    How you win work, where the money leaks, what your people spend their days on, and what you have quietly stopped believing is possible. Not a workshop, not a questionnaire. We sit in the building and ask questions.

  2. 02
    Then: one page shows where AI pays off for you.

    The services you could offer, the speed you could reach, the costs you could cut — and what stays with your people, because your name, your judgement or your relationships are on it. We read it back to you, and you approve or reject each line before anything is built. That page is the plan.

  3. 03
    Then: we prove the first ideas in your business.

    Two or three ideas from the page are tested in your office, with your data, in your own accounts. What works stays. What does not costs you an afternoon and teaches you something.

  4. 04
    Last: your team takes over, and we decide what is next.

    The people who will run what works learn to adjust it without us, and each system gets a named owner on your side. Then we look at the page together and decide whether we stay on as your Head of AI for the months ahead.

Simple to start. Simple to stop.

  • A fixed price for the engagement, quoted in writing after the free first call. One invoice, no day rates, no expenses.
  • After the consulting work, the Head of AI role runs month by month, for as long as it pays off. You can stop with one month’s notice, any month, without giving a reason.
  • The tools themselves are paid from your own accounts, at cost. We never resell them and never add a margin.
  • Any larger system the work points to is quoted on its own, once we have seen the work and never before.
  • If we find early on that AI will not pay off for you, we say so and stop. You pay only for the days already spent.

Small on purpose. Built to run without us.

Dinobridge is a senior team, led by its founder: the people who do the work are the people at your table, with no account manager in between and no junior learning on your data. That is only safe if nothing important depends on us, so nothing does.

Everything is yours from day one.

Every system runs in your own accounts and belongs to you, down to the last file. You pay for the tools directly, never through us. We hold no access you cannot withdraw in an afternoon.

Every system comes with a written handover.

Written the week the system is built, not the week we leave: what it does, how to adjust it, how to switch it off, and what to check when something looks wrong. Written for the person on your team who will own it, and tested by having them use it while we watch.

A named person on your side owns each system.

We do not hand over to “the team”. Before anything is switched on, one of your people is trained on it, has adjusted it themselves, and is named in the handover as its owner.

Response times you can hold us to.

Same working day for anything that is broken, two working days for everything else, and a named window each week when you know where to find us. If we are away for more than a week, you get the dates in advance.

If we stop, nothing stops.

Nothing we build depends on a licence, account or subscription of ours. If we disappeared tomorrow, everything would keep running and your named owners could keep adjusting it. That is the test we design against, and you can put it in the contract.

We work on a mandate, not as staff.

The Head of AI role is an independent mandate with its own deliverables, its own tools and several clients — not employment, not instruction-bound work, and not part of your organisation. If your finance or HR team wants that confirmed in advance, we put it in the contract and are happy to have the status formally determined first.
