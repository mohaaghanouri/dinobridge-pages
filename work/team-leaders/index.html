Team leaders

Every one-to-one starts where the last one ended, and reviews take an afternoon.

One leads eleven people in an engineering organisation, one leads six in customer operations. Both were promoted for being excellent at the job they no longer do, neither was given any training, and both are quietly drowning in the paperwork that comes with managing people.

Read more

Preparation, or rather the lack of it. Eleven one-to-one meetings a fortnight, each of which should begin with “last time you said X, how did that go?” and instead begins with the leader silently trying to remember. Then the writing: feedback that needs to be specific and therefore needs evidence, evidence that lives in scattered memory, and twice a year the review cycle, which eats two full weekends.

The result.

Review season went from two weekends to an afternoon, and one-to-ones that open with a follow-up went from occasional to standard. The team noticed the difference before the leader did. Beyond that, you can see the share of your week actually spent with your team, and whether your feedback has become specific.

What we take off your desk

  • Holding everything you have noticed about your team in one private record you own: what was said, what was agreed, what is still open
  • Making sure every conversation starts where the last one ended, rather than with both people trying to remember
  • Turning what you observed into feedback specific enough to land: the situation, what the person did, what effect it had
  • Keeping track of what you delegated and what became of it
  • Showing you where your time actually goes, against where you believe it goes
  • Assembling a year of your own observations when a review comes round, instead of reconstructing it in November
  • Writing down how the team actually works, so it survives the next person who joins

What you get.

One private record per person

One private file per team member, held by you: what they said they would do, what happened, and your own dated observations. Ninety seconds of dictation after each one-to-one keeps it current. It is the heart of the work and lifts the quality of your management more than anything else, because specific feedback lands and vague feedback does not.

Every conversation starts where the last one ended

Before each one-to-one: last time’s agreements, their status, what is still open, what you said you would follow up on. Thirty seconds of reading replaces four minutes of remembering badly, and the person across the table can tell which one happened.

Feedback that lands

You note “Tuesday, the client escalation, handled it well.” You get back one specific paragraph — the situation, what the person did, what effect it had — built from that note and nothing else. You edit it and deliver it in person.

Where your time actually went

Once a month, the difference between where you believe your week goes and where it went. Both leaders were shocked. One discovered that 8% of their time went to their team and 44% to meetings they had never once questioned attending.

Reviews in an afternoon

At review time, the year’s notes for each person, in order. Two weekends become an afternoon. The reviews are far more specific, because they are built from notes written at the time rather than from what you happen to remember.

Safeguards you can rely on.

Every judgement about a person stays with you, and nobody on your team is watched.

It never rates, never ranks, never predicts who might leave, never suggests who is underperforming, never writes the conclusion of a review. A performance judgement produced by software and signed by a manager is a lie that somebody’s career is then built on.

Nor does it reach into anyone’s work: no counting of output, no reading of messages, no tracking of activity. Both leaders had tools that could do this, and we recommended neither. Surveillance changes what a team tells you long before it changes what they do, and then you are blind in a way you cannot detect.

The person records are your own notes about employees, so GDPR rights apply, including the right of access. Write nothing you would not show the person.

When we advise against it
