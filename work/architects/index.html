Architects

The paperwork gets done the same day. The design and the compliance call stay with you.

A two-person practice doing residential conversions, a sole practitioner working mostly on planning applications and conversions, and a mid-size office where one partner runs all tendering and contract award. All three trained for years to design. All three spend most of their week on the paperwork around it.

For one it is the drawing register: which revision went to whom, and whether the structural engineer has the current one. For another it is the Angebotsvergleich: seven contractor quotes in seven formats, compared line by line in Excel over a weekend. For the third it is the site Protokoll: forty photos and an hour of notes that have to become a document the same evening, or never.

The result.

A weekend of comparing contractor quotes now takes about two hours. Nearly 100% of site Protokolle are written the same day, and no submission has been returned incomplete since the check began. “Which revision is current?” is a two-second question, with an answer you can show.

What stays with you

What you get.

The drawing register

Every drawing, every revision, and who received what and when. Small and unglamorous, it removes a recurring worry: “does the structural engineer have rev C?” becomes a two-second question with an answer you can show.

The quote comparison

Seven quotes in, one comparison out, item by item. Unpriced items are flagged as gaps, not counted as zeros, because the cheapest quote is very often the one that left something out. It flags, and the decision stays with you.

The site Protokoll, the same evening

On site you take photos and talk. That evening the Protokoll is ready: dated, photos referenced, defects listed, responsibilities assigned and deadlines on a reminder list. The document that used to be written at 22:00 or not at all now takes fifteen minutes of editing.

The standards finder

It searches your own library of standards and the Bebauungsplan and returns the passage with its citation. It looks things up and never puts a norm in its own words, because a paraphrased norm is a liability.

The completeness check

Before submission, against the authority’s own list. It catches the missing Nachweis before the Bauamt does, which saves weeks.

Safeguards you can rely on.

Your design stays yours, and so does every compliance decision.

It proposes no massing and no layouts. Software can produce something plan-shaped, but your stamp carries the liability and the design is the whole value of your practice. A design made by software and signed by a person is the worst of both.

On compliance it finds the relevant norm and never rules on it. The sentence it may write is “§ 34 BauGB, paragraph 2, may apply here — check.” The sentence it will never write is “this complies.”

Before you decide.

What we need from you

Talking on site instead of writing at night. You take photos and dictate while you are there, then spend fifteen minutes that evening editing the Protokoll instead of two hours writing it.

When we advise against it

When a practice wants software that generates designs. That is a different profession from ours, and for a three-person office doing conversions it solves a problem they do not have while adding liability they cannot carry.
