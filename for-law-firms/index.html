Professional secrecy, covered in writing before we start.

Two documents, because German professional law requires both and only one of them is about data protection.

  • A data processing agreement (AVV) under Art. 28 GDPR, signed before we see anything.
  • A confidentiality undertaking in Textform under § 43e BRAO, in which we are expressly instructed on the criminal consequences of a breach under § 203 StGB, and which names everyone at Dinobridge who will work on your matter. Each of them is bound by it in writing before they see anything, and nobody is added without a written amendment beforehand.

We know § 43e is a separate duty from Art. 28, and that you must be able to show you chose your provider with care. You get the paperwork for that at the start, without having to ask.

You decide how long the record is kept, and you can export all of it at any time without asking us. In a law firm, AI without a full record is not usable, whatever else it can do.

Access follows the permissions you already have: if a fee-earner cannot open a matter today, nothing we build lets them open it tomorrow. Our own access is named in writing, limited to the work, and withdrawn by you at the end of each phase. We keep no permanent access of our own.

A lawyer decides every change.

  1. It arrives, is recognised, linked to its matter and its earlier drafts, and filed.
  2. A summary sheet is made, every entry linked to the page and paragraph it came from.
  3. It is compared with your playbook — your own written positions — and the differences are marked.
  4. A Word document with tracked changes is prepared, each suggestion with its reason as a comment: which of your positions it comes from, or which risk it addresses.
  5. A lawyer opens it in Word and accepts or rejects each change, one by one.
  6. Every one of those decisions is recorded.

Nothing between the first step and the fifth leaves the firm, and nothing after the fifth happens without a person.

Every statement can be checked in seconds.

Four safeguards, and none of them asks you to take the machine at its word.

  • Nothing is stated without its source. Every fact carries the page and paragraph it came from, so checking it takes four seconds — a fact you cannot check is worse than no fact, because it looks like knowledge.
  • Nothing is suggested without a reason. A markup with no stated basis cannot be reviewed, so the system cannot produce one.
  • The comparison is with your own written positions, not with a machine’s idea of market standard. Where a position is not written down, the system says so rather than guessing.
  • Nothing is paraphrased. For statutes and clauses you get the exact wording and where it stands, because a paraphrased provision is a liability.

And by design, nothing reaches a counterparty, a client or a colleague without a person accepting a specific change.

Before anything starts: the NDA, the AVV, the § 43e undertaking, and one page stating what we will access and for how long. Then filing, then the summary sheet and the deadline diary, then your playbook, written down properly with you.

The playbook is the hardest and most valuable part, and most firms have never written theirs down. The first markup comes last, because it depends on the playbook, and the playbook is your work, not ours. Anyone promising you a markup in the second week has never written a playbook with a firm.

It works inside the tools your firm already uses — Outlook, Word, SharePoint, your calendar and, in most cases, your document management system — so your people stay in Word rather than in someone else’s portal. If your system cannot be connected, you hear that on the first call rather than in the third week.

For every piece, a named person in your firm is trained on it and has changed a rule in it themselves before it goes live.
