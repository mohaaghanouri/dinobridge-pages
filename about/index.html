About us

What stands behind Dinobridge.

Most AI projects fail for one of two reasons. Either the person who understood the business could not build, so the thing that got built solved the wrong problem — or the person who could build did not understand the business, so it solved a real problem nobody had.

Years of professional engineering sit behind Dinobridge, spent building AI systems in production: the kind other people depend on, that survive an audit, a handover and colleagues who never asked for the tool.

Dinobridge is owned and led by Moha Aghanoori, and this page is the case for why that combination is the qualification rather than a compromise.

Why that combination is the qualification.

  1. AI engineering, not prompt-writing.

    Retrieval pipelines, agents, model and prompt versioning, deployment, and evaluation harnesses that decide what is allowed to reach production. This is the part that separates a demo from a system your team can rely on when nobody is watching it.

  2. Business operations.

    Years of it inside organisations where the AI had to work for colleagues who never asked for it, survive a handover, and answer to an auditor. That is a different discipline from making something impressive, and it is the one that decides whether a system is still in use a year later.

  3. Regulated workflows.

    Contracts under confidentiality, with permissions and an audit trail as first-class requirements rather than a later phase. It is why the law-firm work starts with what may not leave the building rather than with what the model can do.

  4. Leadership.

    The people who commissioned those systems were the people accountable for the function. Explaining a trade-off to a decision-maker in their own vocabulary — and being told no, and saying no — is most of the job.

  5. Implementation.

    Every system described on this site was built by the person who sat in the room where the problem was described. There is nobody to hand it to.

The honest part.

Moha Aghanoori holds a full-time engineering role building production AI, and that role is where most of the production experience on this site comes from — it is the reason we can tell you what survives an audit rather than guessing. It also means capacity is real and limited, and you should know its shape before we talk rather than after.

  • We take a small number of clients at a time. The line at the top of the home page says whether there is room.
  • The shape of an engagement — which days, on site or on video, over how many weeks — is agreed with you in writing after the first call and before there is a contract. We will never tell you a day is available and then move it.
  • The Head of AI role is a named window every week, agreed before we start.
  • Client work and employed work are kept strictly separate. No client’s material has ever touched another engagement, and none ever will.
  • If what you need is more than that, you hear it before there is a contract. It has happened.

What we will not do.

Build the button that takes the decision away. Automate anything where your name, your licence or your signature is on the result. Sell you a system when a calendar, a form or a shared spreadsheet would fix it — that has cost us work, and it is most of the reason clients introduce us to other owners.
